# AI entertainment startup: landscape check

## The idea, as understood

A startup whose product is AI-generated entertainment: short video, playable worlds and game assets, songs, interactive characters, and episodic or interactive stories, sold to consumers or as tools to studios and creators. Researched as a category across those five sub-segments, since the request named no specific product.

## Who is already there

Every sub-segment has a same-thing occupant with published pricing.

| Sub-segment | Occupants and pricing | Closeness |
|---|---|---|
| Video | Runway $12 to $76/mo; Pika $8 to $95; Luma $25 to $300; Google Veo in AI Pro $19.99; Hedra API at 1.22 cents/second | Same thing |
| Playable worlds and game tools | Google Project Genie (Ultra tier, 60-second sessions); Decart ($300M raise); Rosebud $15 to $100; Scenario $15 to $75; Roblox Cube | Same thing |
| Music | Suno $8 to $24 with download caps; Udio (UMG walled garden); ElevenLabs Music $6 to $99, film and TV excluded | Same thing |
| Characters | Character.AI $9.99 (in Chapter 11); Replika, Talkie, Nomi; 337 revenue-generating apps per Appfigures | Same thing |
| Narrative and shows | AI Dungeon (2019), Hidden Door (licensed worlds), Fable Showrunner (Amazon stake) | Same thing |

All prices are vendor pages. The absorbers that are shipping are distribution platforms, not model labs: Google and YouTube (Veo free in Shorts, Lyria 3 in Gemini), ByteDance (Seedance 2.0 inside CapCut, US since April 2026), Adobe (multi-model generation inside Premiere), Roblox (in-game object generation, prompt-to-scene as stated goal), Epic (AI NPCs in UEFN), Nvidia ACE (shipped in PUBG and inZOI), and Spotify plus UMG (licensed remix add-on announced).

Gaps no occupant covers: no product spans more than two sub-segments with one persistent world; generated playable worlds hold for about a minute; rights-cleared music for film, TV and studio games is carved out of every self-serve license.

## The graveyard

The graveyard is fresh and independently sourced.

- **Sora** (OpenAI): launched September 2025, shut April 2026. WSJ-sourced reporting called it "a money pit that nobody was using" at about $1M a day. Appfigures: 9.6M downloads, $1.4M lifetime consumer revenue. Disney's $1B deal died with it.
- **Character.AI**: 20M monthly users, a $2.7B Google license, then Chapter 11 citing costs and litigation. Disney bought assets for $20M. Two hours a day of inference did not fit a $9.99 subscription, and wrongful-death suits plus state enforcement followed.
- **Companions**: Soulmate, Forever Voices and Dot are gone. Inflection ($1.3B raised) became a $650M Microsoft acquihire. Replika survived only after a regulator forced removal of its core paid feature.
- **Music**: Udio was forced into a DRM walled garden by UMG. Suno survives on $650M-plus of capital and licenses while UMG and Sony sue again over 60,202 works.
- **Pivots**: Inworld (best-funded AI NPC company) to voice infrastructure. Runway's $315M Series E is earmarked for robotics and medicine world models.

The reasons still hold: inference cost rises with engagement, the most engaged users want content app stores forbid, and rights holders use litigation to set terms and then license only the best-capitalized players.

## The economics

Consumers do not pay for generation. Sora earned about $0.15 per download. Companion apps earn $1.18 per download, but the category is about $120M a year across 337 apps with 89 percent going to the top tenth. Runway's list price implies $7 to $17 per minute of usable video. The only at-scale video revenue is Kuaishou's Kling, roughly $500M annualized, under 1 percent of group sales, funded by billions in capex.

Music is the one segment with a plausible consumer price (Suno claims $300M ARR at about $12.50 a month, vendor numbers). The cost of entry is a label license, which the majors have granted only to parties they first sued, and Google sells full songs by API at $0.08 each.

Studios and labels are a tollbooth, not a market. Disney sued Midjourney, invested in Sora, watched it die, then bought Character.AI's assets out of bankruptcy.

## The commodity floor

Roughly 85 to 95 percent of the consumer tasks are free or bundled. Veo 3 Fast is free inside YouTube Shorts. Gemini Omni video and Lyria 3.5 ship in every paid Google plan. Meta Vibes is a free AI video feed. Suno gives 50 free credits a day. SillyTavern (35k stars) plus any model is a complete free character stack with memory. ComfyUI (145k stars) wires Wan2.2, HunyuanVideo, LTX, YuE and ACE-Step together on a consumer GPU. Frontier chat models write interactive fiction unaided.

The thin layers are episodic AI shows (about 60 percent free, occupied by Showrunner and Vibes) and real-time playable worlds (about 50 percent, occupied by Google, Decart and Tencent). The sellable delta is licensed IP, rights-cleared output, and B2B engines for studios.

## What would have to be true

1. **A consumer segment pays more than inference costs.** Settled by any product disclosing gross margin on its free-to-paid funnel. Sora and Character.AI falsified it at the top of the market; Suno is the one vendor-claimed counterexample and has not disclosed margins.
2. **Rights holders license a new entrant on terms that leave a business.** Settled by one major label or studio signing a startup it has not sued. Licenses so far went to Suno, Udio and Spotify only after litigation.
3. **A persistent cross-segment world is reachable.** Settled by a generated world holding state for an hour with multiplayer. Genie 3 holds a few minutes. Falsified for now.
4. **The studio B2B wedge is real.** Settled by two studio contracts for rights-cleared generation with disclosed value. Lionsgate and Runway exists but is undisclosed. Open.
5. **A distribution platform does not bundle the feature first.** Settled by watching YouTube, CapCut, Roblox and Spotify over two quarters. Each has already shipped or announced it.
